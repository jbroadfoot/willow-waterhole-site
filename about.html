Our Mission

Preserve, restore, and enhance Willow Waterhole Greenway to reduce flooding, protect habitat, and connect people through nature and community.

What We Do

Four kinds of work, one footprint.

Flood protection

The greenway is a working stormwater detention system that helps protect surrounding neighborhoods during major storms.

Habitat restoration

We actively manage prairie, wetland, and woodland habitats that support hundreds of plant and animal species.

Recreation & access

About 8 miles of ADA-accessible trails, six lakes, and gathering spaces, free and open to all every day.

Education & community

Bird surveys, fishing clinics, volunteer days, and events that connect people to nature and each other.

Greenway History

From stormwater basin to community treasure.

In 2001, as Harris County began designing flood-control detention basins along Brays Bayou, a group of neighbors saw something more. They founded the Willow Waterhole Greenspace Conservancy with an ambitious goal: build a major public park on top of working flood infrastructure, and let one piece of ground do two things at once.

Two decades of volunteers, partners, and community investment made it real. What was once bare engineered earth is now 291 acres of lakes, trails, native prairie, public art, and gathering places, one of southwest Houston's most loved green spaces, built by the people who live beside it.

Flood Control

How the lakes reduce flooding.

Every lake you walk beside is doing a second job.

The six connected lakes of Willow Waterhole are a working stormwater detention system, engineered by the Harris County Flood Control District as part of Project Brays, a regional effort with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers to reduce flood risk across the Brays Bayou watershed. When heavy rain falls, the lakes fill, holding water that would otherwise surge into neighborhood streets, then release it slowly as the bayou recedes. Together the basins can hold roughly 608 million gallons.

The system reduces flood risk. It cannot prevent every flood. Storms larger than the design event can still overwhelm any detention system. But in storm after storm, these basins have quietly done exactly what they were built to do.

The Harris County Flood Control District engineers and maintains the infrastructure. The Conservancy stewards the living greenspace on top of it. That partnership is the whole idea of Willow Waterhole: protection and beauty on the same ground.

Our Partners

We don't do this alone.

Willow Waterhole exists thanks to a partnership of public agencies, foundations, and community organizations, each with a distinct role.

Public Partners

Harris County Flood Control District

Engineered and maintains the detention basins the greenway is built on; manages the prairie preserve under a formal management plan.

Houston Parks & Recreation

The city partner for parkland, programming, and public access across the greenway.

City of Houston & TIRZ 20

Civic partners supporting infrastructure, access, and neighborhood investment around the park. TIRZ 20 annexed Willow Waterhole Greenway in late 2023.

Brays Oaks Management District

A district partner supporting safety, beautification, and community connection in southwest Houston.

Texas Parks & Wildlife

Stocked four lakes with bass, catfish, and sunfish, and funded the Prairie Boardwalk through a 2023 grant.

Harris County Precinct One

A county partner supporting community investment and public programming near the greenway.
