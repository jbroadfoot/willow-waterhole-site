FAQs

Good questions, quick answers.

Everything visitors ask most, in one place.

What are the hours, and does it cost anything?
The park is open dawn to dusk, every day of the year. Admission is always free.
Where should I park?
Four lots serve the park: the Dryad Drive Lot (5300 Dryad Dr, the main entrance, closest to Westbury Lake), two Clematis Lane lots (11200 and 11300 blocks, closest to Willow and Heron Lakes), and the Gasmer Drive Lot (4700 block, closest to Prairie Lake and the boardwalk). See Getting Here & Parking for which lot fits your visit.
Are there restrooms?
No. There are no restrooms on site, so plan ahead. The nearby Dr. Walker Library offers indoor space and restrooms.
Can I bring my dog?
Yes. Dogs are welcome on leash. Please keep them leashed at all times and use the waste stations throughout the park.
Can I fish?
Yes. From 2016 to 2019, the Texas Parks & Wildlife Department stocked four lakes (Westbury, Willow, Prairie, and Triangle) with bass, catfish, and sunfish. Carp and other invasive non-native fish can also be found and should never be released back into the water. Fishing poles are available to borrow; email ahead to arrange. See the full lakes guide for each lake's amenities.
Can I launch a canoe, kayak, or paddleboard?
Yes. Most people use Westbury Lake since it is the largest. A pier for fishing and launching is located at the northwest corner of Westbury Lake near the Westbury Little League field. Email info@willowwaterhole.org for more information.
Are there alligators?
No. Old warning signs at Westbury Lake were installed years ago by HCFCD in case alligators ever arrived. None did. The Conservancy removed the signs because they were giving the wrong impression and scaring visitors.
Is the park accessible?
Yes. About 8 miles of ADA-accessible paved trails connect all six lakes, and the fishing pier at Westbury Lake is ADA-compliant. The Prairie Boardwalk is also fully accessible.
When is the best time to see birds?
Early morning and late afternoon, year-round. Spring and fall migration bring the biggest variety. A monthly bird survey runs the third Saturday of every month (except December), open to all skill levels. See Birds & Wildlife.
What native trees grow at the Greenway?
Only native trees are planted. Common species include live oak, water oak, swamp chestnut oak, red oak, bald cypress, river birch, pecan, hickory, sweetgum, cedar elm, eastern red cedar, Mexican sycamore, Mexican buckeye, Mexican plum, loblolly pine, redbud, wax myrtle, hackberry, willow, southern magnolia, yaupon, and white fringe. Most are planted November through March by volunteers, with many donated by Trees for Houston.
How often is the Greenway mowed?
The Harris County Flood Control District mows the lake banks and areas without amenities three times a year: mid-April, mid-August, and mid-November. The City of Houston Parks and Recreation Department mows sections with amenities every two to three weeks depending on the season.
Is the park really a flood-control project?
Yes. The six lakes are working stormwater detention basins engineered by the Harris County Flood Control District as part of Project Brays. During flood events, overflow from Willow Waterhole Bayou is diverted into the basin complex, held until the storm passes, then slowly released downstream into Brays Bayou. Together the basins hold 608 million gallons. The Conservancy stewards the greenspace built on top. Read how the lakes reduce flooding.
When was construction started?
Construction began in 2003 after the Harris County Flood Control District purchased 291 acres in southwest Houston with funding from the Brays Bayou Federal Flood Damage Reduction Project. The six detention basins were completed in 2019. Read more about the history of the Greenway.
What is the size and capacity of the basin complex?
The six completed lake compartments vary widely in size and together are capable of holding 608 million gallons of stormwater, roughly the equivalent of six Houston Astrodomes.
How did the Conservancy originate?
In 2001, as the Harris County Flood Control District began designing the detention basins, the Brays Bayou Association founded the Willow Waterhole Greenspace Conservancy. The nonprofit was dedicated to creating a major public park on top of the flood-control complex, in partnership with neighborhood residents, environmental organizations, HCFCD, Harris County Precinct 1, Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, the City of Houston, and the Houston Parks Board.
What events does the Conservancy host each year?
Ten to fifteen Conservation Workdays are held every year, powered by volunteers. The Greenway also hosts an annual music festival, scholastic cross-country meets, community fun runs, and fishing clinics throughout the year. See all upcoming events.
Can I hold an event at the Greenway?
Yes. The Bob Schwartz Gazebo (5300 Dryad Dr) and the Rafferty Memorial Oak area (11350 Ricecrest St) are available for small events like birthday parties, scout ceremonies, and meetings on a first-come, first-served basis. They cannot be reserved for small gatherings. For events with more than 50 people, the Bob Schwartz Gazebo can be reserved through the City of Houston Parks and Recreation Department. A permit and fee apply based on group size and nonprofit status. Email info@willowwaterhole.org for guidance.
How do I become a member?
The Conservancy welcomes new members and depends on generous visitors and community residents for help with conservation, maintenance, events, and projects. Visit Friends of Willow Waterhole or email membership@willowwaterhole.org.
Can I volunteer?
Absolutely. The Conservancy relies on volunteers for conservation workdays, community events, and special projects. Workdays run monthly and are open to individuals, groups, scouts, and corporate teams. Start at Get Involved or email info@willowwaterhole.org.
Can my child do a Scout or community service project here?
Yes. More than 80 Eagle Scout and Gold Award projects have been completed at the Greenway: benches, birdhouses, trail features, kiosks, and more. Email info@willowwaterhole.org to discuss your project idea.
Can someone from the Conservancy speak at our meeting?
Yes. The Conservancy has speakers available on topics including the Greenway's history, flood control, native prairie, and wildlife. Email info@willowwaterhole.org to set something up.
