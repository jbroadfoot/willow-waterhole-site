The Wildflower That Redrew the Plan

Willow Waterhole was supposed to be simpler than it turned out to be.

In the planning for Project Brays, engineers identified a stretch of land along Willow Waterhole Bayou near Westbury as a good site for stormwater detention. The ground was flat, it was close to the bayou, and it was large enough to hold a serious volume of water. The plan was to excavate.

Then someone found a plant.

Texas prairie dawn, Hymenoxys texana, is a small yellow wildflower that grows in only a few counties on the upper Texas coast. It is federally endangered. It is also easy to miss: a few inches tall, blooming for a short window in early spring, growing in the sparse patches where the soil is too poor for anything more aggressive to crowd it out.

Finding it on the site meant excavation could not simply proceed.

What followed changed the shape of the project. The Flood Control District and the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service developed a conservation plan, first written in 2013, to protect what turned out to be a genuine remnant of native coastal prairie: ground that had never been plowed, never been developed, and still held the seed bank of the ecosystem that covered this part of Texas before Houston existed.

The work was not glamorous. Crews removed encroaching trees so sunlight could reach the ground and wake seeds that had been waiting in the soil. Invasive vegetation was cleared. Fences went up around the known populations of endangered flowers. Native prairie plants and rare wildflowers were transplanted in, and seed from other coastal prairie species was sown.

Today the prairie preserve covers 15 acres and supports 265 native plant species, three of them protected. Texas prairie dawn is the endangered one. Houston camphor daisy and Texas windmill grass, both threatened, grow there too.

The one-third mile boardwalk that now crosses the preserve exists because of what that survey turned up. So does the fact that the Greenway is not simply a set of holes in the ground with mown grass around them.

It is worth remembering the sequence. The flood infrastructure did not make room for the prairie as a courtesy. The prairie was already there, and the law required the project to work around it.

Next time you visit
The boardwalk is the only way to see the preserve up close without walking on it. Texas prairie dawn blooms in early spring and is genuinely hard to spot. Look low, in the open patches where the grass thins out.
