Greenway Improvements · April 2026

Celebrating the Partners Behind the Boardwalk Trail

On April 8, the Willow Waterhole Greenspace Conservancy hosted a thank-you tour for the organizations whose generosity made the new Boardwalk Trail at the Native Prairie Preserve a reality.

The gathering brought together an all-star lineup of funders and partners: the Texas Parks and Wildlife Department, the Kinder Foundation®, the Brown and Cullen Foundations, and the River Oaks Garden Club. Also in attendance were representatives from the Harris County Flood Control District and the engineering firm HR Green.

Why the Boardwalk Matters

The Boardwalk Trail allows visitors to experience a rare native coastal prairie, of which only 1% remains today due to urbanization and agricultural use. When the site was identified by HCFCD, more than 265 native plant species were found within the 15-acre preserve, including one endangered species (Texas Prairie Dawn) and two threatened species (Houston Camphor Daisy and Texas Windmill Grass).

The ADA-accessible trail features educational signage, a viewing platform, a resting area, and an outdoor classroom, allowing visitors to experience the prairie without a single footstep on its fragile ground.

Completing a Larger Vision

The Boardwalk Trail completes a series of major accessibility improvements made possible by the Conservancy’s $12 million Discover Your Greenway capital campaign. Other projects included about eight miles of ADA-accessible trails, four new parking areas, five safe street crossings, a fishing pier, wayfinding signage, benches and tables, security enhancements, and hundreds of new trees.

Thank you to all the donors, volunteers, advisors, community leaders, and contractors who made these projects a reality.
